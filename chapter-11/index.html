EIDL in Chapter 11 and Subchapter V

Chapter 11 (and its small-business variant, Subchapter V) restructures rather than discharges immediately. EIDL claims are usually treated as general unsecured and paid per the confirmed plan, with discharge on plan confirmation (entity) or plan completion (individual Sub V).

Subchapter V (small-business reorganization)

Subchapter V was created by the Small Business Reorganization Act of 2019 to give small businesses a faster, cheaper Chapter 11. The current debt limit (post-CARES sunset, January 2026) is approximately $3.024 million in noncontingent liquidated debt. EIDL plus other debt under that ceiling: Sub V is available.

Sub V typically gives EIDL claims:

  • Pro-rata distribution as general unsecured under the plan
  • 3-5 year plan period
  • Discharge of remaining unsecured debt at plan completion (individual debtor) or plan confirmation (entity debtor)

Standard Chapter 11

For larger cases above the Sub V threshold: standard Chapter 11. EIDL claims are still typically general unsecured and paid per plan. Plans must satisfy the absolute priority rule (or qualify for the new-value exception).

Common pitfalls

  • Adequate protection demands: SBA may file motions seeking adequate protection of its UCC-1 collateral if the business continues operating.
  • SBA's vote: SBA can vote against the plan if the unsecured pool's recovery is too low. The cramdown standard under § 1129(b) governs.
  • Loan-document subordination: COVID-EIDL loan documents include a subordination provision; review with counsel before assuming priority.

Sub V loan-balance trap

Sub V eligibility is measured at the petition date. If you took a $1M EIDL plus other liabilities exceeding $3.024M total, Sub V is unavailable and standard Chapter 11 is required — significantly more expensive.
