SBA Collections, Treasury Offset, and DOJ Referrals

When an EIDL goes into default, the SBA does not file lawsuits itself in most cases. Instead, the loan moves through an administrative collection chain that ends with Treasury referral and, for fraud cases, DOJ.

The collection chain

  1. SBA servicing center (e.g., Birmingham, AL) sends default notices and demands payment.
  2. SBA charges off the loan after typically 90+ days of nonpayment.
  3. Referral to Treasury Cross-Servicing (Bureau of the Fiscal Service) for collection.
  4. Treasury Offset Program (TOP): federal tax refunds, federal salary, and Social Security benefits (over a protected floor) can be offset to apply against the debt.
  5. Private Collection Agencies (PCAs) may receive the debt for active collection on Treasury's behalf.
  6. DOJ referral for high-dollar or fraud-flagged cases — civil suit and/or criminal prosecution.

Treasury Offset Program (TOP) detail

TOP is the most common collection method for delinquent SBA debts:

  • Federal tax refunds: 100% offset until debt is satisfied
  • Federal salary: up to 15% of disposable pay
  • Social Security retirement / disability: up to 15%, with a $750/month protected floor (subject to anti-hardship provisions)
  • SSI: NOT subject to offset
  • Federal contractor payments: up to 100% if you're a contractor with the government

How bankruptcy stops collections

The automatic stay under 11 U.S.C. § 362 stops:

  • SBA charge-off and demand letters
  • Treasury Offset Program seizures (you may need to notify Treasury via Form 1024 to stop active offsets)
  • Private Collection Agency calls and letters
  • DOJ civil collection actions (criminal cases proceed despite the stay; § 362(b)(1))

Refund offsets that occurred before the petition: not automatically recoverable; the offset already happened and the SBA holds the funds. Offsets that occur after the petition (in violation of the stay): typically recoverable as an § 362(k) violation, with damages and attorney fees available to the debtor.

DOJ referral signals

Watch for these — they indicate a fraud-based AP is likely:

  • SBA OIG subpoena or civil investigative demand (CID)
  • OIG interview request (in person or by phone)
  • U.S. Attorney's Office contact letter
  • Filing of a False Claims Act qui tam against you (rare but increasing for EIDL)
