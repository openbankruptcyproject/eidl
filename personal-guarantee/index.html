EIDL Personal Guarantee — The $200K Threshold

The single biggest factor in whether an EIDL borrower has personal liability. For COVID-era EIDL: loans under $200,000 generally required no personal guarantee; loans at or above $200,000 required one from any owner of 20% or more of the business.

How the threshold worked

Per the SBA's COVID-EIDL terms (March 2020 forward), the personal-guarantee requirement scaled with loan size:

Loan amountPG required?
$0 - $25,000No PG, no collateral
$25,001 - $200,000No PG, but UCC-1 lien on business assets
$200,001 and upPG required from each 20%+ owner; UCC-1 + best-efforts real-estate collateral if available

What no-PG means for individual bankruptcy

If the EIDL is the obligation of an LLC or corporation and no PG was signed, the loan is the entity's debt only. The owner's individual Chapter 7 or 13 filing has no effect on it. The SBA can pursue the entity's assets but cannot reach the owner's personal assets.

This is the most common surprise for COVID-EIDL borrowers under $200K: they don't need to file personal bankruptcy at all to walk away from the loan. They can dissolve the entity, return any remaining assets to the SBA per the lien, and the loan becomes uncollectable against them personally.

What PG means for individual bankruptcy

If you signed a PG on a $200K+ EIDL, the loan is also your debt. The SBA can pursue you personally regardless of what happens to the business.

In Chapter 7: the PG-backed liability is unsecured (the SBA's collateral is the business's, not yours) and is discharged with other unsecured debt — subject to fraud carve-outs.

In Chapter 13: the PG liability is treated like other unsecured claims and gets pro-rata distribution under your plan. Discharge at plan completion.

Verifying your PG status

Check your loan documents:

  • SBA Form 2202 (Schedule of Liabilities) — shows whether collateral was pledged
  • SBA Form 148 / 148L — the personal-guarantee form itself
  • SBA Form 4 (Application for Business Loan) for Disaster Assistance loans
  • UCC-1 financing statement — filed with the state for loans $25K+

You can request your full SBA loan file via FOIA from the SBA Office of Disaster Assistance (ODA) if you don't have it.
