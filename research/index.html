EIDL Discharge — Empirical Research

Open dataset and analysis of EIDL and SBA-loan discharge proceedings in U.S. bankruptcy courts. Filing volume, fraud-AP rates, and post-COVID trends. Methodology and data versioned in the public repository.

Status: dataset assembly in progress

The COVID-EIDL portfolio — approximately 4 million loans totaling $390+ billion — is now five years past origination. Default and bankruptcy patterns are emerging but the full empirical picture takes time to develop. This research page tracks what we are pulling from public bankruptcy court records (CourtListener, RECAP, PACER) plus SBA OIG public reporting.

What we are tracking

  • EIDL/SBA fraud APs: § 523(a)(2)/(4)/(6) adversary proceedings filed by SBA, DOJ, or DOJ Civil Division naming SBA as the holder. Fraud-AP volume is the strongest signal of OIG referral pipeline.
  • Sub V plan treatment of EIDL: how Subchapter V plans classify and pay EIDL claims, and what recovery rates look like.
  • Personal-guarantee cases: Chapter 7 individual filings where EIDL is listed; what fraction draws an SBA AP vs. routine discharge.
  • Treasury Offset stays: § 362(k) damages awards for post-petition TOP offsets.

Methodology (planned)

  1. Identify defendants matching SBA / U.S. Small Business Administration / United States of America (where SBA is the lending agency) in CourtListener AP filings.
  2. Classify by chapter (7, 11, 13, Sub V), § 523 ground (a)(2)(A), (a)(2)(B), (a)(4), (a)(6), and outcome (pending, dismissed, judgment for defendant, judgment for plaintiff, settled).
  3. Cross-reference with SBA OIG investigation public listings where available.
  4. Publish dataset under CC BY 4.0 with versioned methodology.

Why this matters

The COVID-EIDL portfolio is the largest disaster-loan portfolio in SBA history. Default and discharge patterns over the next 3-5 years will define the practical answer to a question many borrowers and counsel are asking now: can a EIDL loan be discharged in bankruptcy? The doctrinal answer is yes (subject to fraud carve-outs). The empirical answer — what actually happens in court — is what this research will track.
